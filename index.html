Runway Gen-4.5: What's New, How to Access It, and What It Costs

Runway Gen-4.5 arrived on December 1, 2025 with a 720p ceiling, a 12-credit-per-second meter and a Standard-plan gate. The specifics, without the launch-reel gloss.

The short versionGen-4.5 is for people already paying for a Runway Standard plan or higher who want the current top-rated text-to-video model and can work inside a 10-second, 720p envelope. It is not for anyone who needs long-form output, a desktop app, or a model they can call from their own backend, because the spec sheet lists web as the only platform. The one caveat worth knowing before you budget: the cost is quoted in credits per second, not dollars, so your real cost per finished clip depends on your plan's credit price and on how many takes you discard. What Runway actually shipped on December 1

The announcement went up on December 1, 2025. Runway's claim for Gen-4.5 is the top slot on the Artificial Analysis Text to Video benchmark with 1,247 Elo points, measured against the leaderboard as of November 30, 2025. The company attributes the jump to pre-training data efficiency and post-training technique rather than to a bigger model, and says the work runs on NVIDIA Hopper and Blackwell hardware. The part that matters commercially is quieter: Runway states that Gen-4.5 keeps the speed of Gen-4 and is available at comparable pricing across all subscription plans, so this is not a premium tier that costs extra on top of what you already pay. Existing control modes such as Keyframes and Video to Video were described as coming to Gen-4.5, not shipping with it.

The spec sheet, line by line

Runway's help centre publishes hard numbers, and they are narrower than the launch page suggests. Gen-4.5 needs a Standard plan or higher. It bills 12 credits per second of output. Supported durations run from 2 to 10 seconds. Output resolution is 720p, at either 24 or 25 frames per second, and web is the only listed platform. Text to Video accepts one aspect ratio, 16:9 at 1280x720. Image to Video is more flexible and adds 9:16 at 720x1280, 1:1 at 960x960, 4:3 at 1104x832, 3:4 at 832x1104 and 21:9 at 1584x672. Explore Mode supports unlimited generations. Those numbers are the reason to read the help centre rather than the blog post: a 10-second cap and a 720p ceiling change what kinds of projects this model can finish.

Finding the model inside the app

Access is not automatic from a menu you already know. Runway's own instructions are to open Apps View and type Gen-4.5 into the search menu at the top of the screen, or to pick it from the Tool Mode model selector with Video selected. From there, Text to Video needs only a prompt. Image to Video wants an image dragged into the prompt window first, and Runway's guidance is that the prompt should then describe motion rather than the scene, since the scene is already in the picture. For pure text prompts, describe both the visual elements and the motion. Gen-4.5 is tuned for sequenced instructions, so camera choreography, timing of events and atmospheric changes can all sit in one prompt instead of being split across takes.

Turning credits into a real cost per clip

Twelve credits per second is a clean number that hides a messy one. A 10-second generation costs 120 credits before you have decided whether the take is usable. Runway's spec page quotes the credit rate but not a credit-to-currency conversion, so the only honest way to budget is to take your plan's credit price from the pricing page and multiply. Then divide by your hit rate. Pick one genuinely hard shot, a person turning their head or a hand gripping an object, run it five times on the same prompt and count how many outputs you would ship. If two of five survive, your effective cost is 600 credits per usable clip, not 120. That single number predicts a production week far better than any benchmark position does.

Where else Gen-4.5 turns up

Runway is not the only front door. Adobe lists Gen-4.5 as a partner model inside Firefly, available through Firefly Generate Video, Image to Video and Firefly Boards, with Runway Aleph 2.0 handling video editing there. Aleph's pitch is keyframe editing: change one frame to swap an object, restyle a shot or adjust the camera angle, and let the change propagate through the clip. If you already pay for Firefly, that route may be cheaper than adding a Runway subscription for occasional use. What you actually get

Ten seconds, hard cap

Supported durations run 2 to 10 seconds. Anything longer is an editing job across multiple generations, which is where character and lighting consistency usually starts to slip.

720p and nothing higher

Output is 720p at 24 or 25 fps. Image to Video offers six aspect ratios including 9:16 and 21:9; Text to Video is 16:9 at 1280x720 only.

Standard plan or higher

The model is gated by subscription tier, not by waitlist. Runway states pricing is comparable across plans to Gen-4, so this is not an upcharge on top of your existing tier.

Web only, for now

Platform availability on the spec sheet reads web. There is no desktop app listed, and the spec page says nothing about calling Gen-4.5 from your own code.

Getting a first clip out

  1. Check your plan tier
    Gen-4.5 requires Standard or higher. If you are on a lower tier the model will not appear in the selector at all, which reads like a rollout delay but is a billing gate.
  2. Search Apps View for Gen-4.5
    Open Apps View and type Gen-4.5 into the search bar at the top, or choose it from the Tool Mode model selector with Video selected. Both routes land in the same prompt window.
  3. Write motion, not just scene
    For Image to Video, drop the image in first and describe what moves. For Text to Video, describe both the visuals and the motion, and put camera choreography in the same prompt.
  4. Run one hard shot five times
    Count usable outputs before you commit a budget. At 12 credits per second, your cost per keeper is the credit rate divided by that hit rate, not the rate itself.

FAQ

What is new in Runway Gen-4.5?

Runway positions it as a step up in motion quality, prompt adherence and visual fidelity over Gen-4, claiming the top position on the Artificial Analysis Text to Video leaderboard with 1,247 Elo points as of November 30, 2025. Crucially, Runway says it keeps Gen-4's speed and sits at comparable pricing across plans rather than launching as a costlier tier.

How much does Runway Gen-4.5 cost?

The spec sheet lists 12 credits per second of generated video, with a Standard plan or higher required. Runway does not publish a credit-to-dollar conversion on that page, so check the pricing page for what a credit costs on your plan and multiply by clip length and by the number of takes you expect to discard.

How long can a Gen-4.5 clip be?

Between 2 and 10 seconds. That is a hard limit on the spec sheet, not a soft default, so anything longer has to be assembled from several generations in an editor. Plan your shot list around 10-second beats rather than expecting to extend a single generation indefinitely.

What resolution does it output?

720p, at 24 or 25 frames per second. Text to Video is restricted to 16:9 at 1280x720. Image to Video adds 9:16 at 720x1280, 1:1 at 960x960, 4:3 at 1104x832, 3:4 at 832x1104 and 21:9 at 1584x672, which makes the image path the better route for vertical social output.

Can I use Gen-4.5 without a Runway subscription?

Adobe lists Gen-4.5 as a partner model inside Firefly, reachable through Firefly Generate Video, Image to Video and Firefly Boards, so a Firefly subscription is one alternative path. If you want neither subscription, Kyncept runs Veo 3.1 for text-to-video and image-to-video in the browser with nothing to install.

Does Gen-4.5 support Keyframes or Video to Video?

Not at announcement. Runway's launch post said existing control modes including Image to Video, Keyframes and Video to Video would be brought to Gen-4.5, and the help centre spec sheet at the time listed Text to Video and Image to Video only. Check the current spec page before planning around a control mode.
